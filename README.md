## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Farmers often depend on intermediaries to sell their produce, reducing their profit margins. At the same time, consumers face fluctuating prices, and bulk buyers struggle to find reliable suppliers. Poor demand prediction, inefficient transportation, and inadequate storage facilities can lead to food wastage.

Key Problems:

1.Limited access to customers and large-scale buyers.
2.Lack of real-time information about market prices.
3.Food wastage due to poor demand planning and storage.
4.High transportation costs and delayed deliveries.
5.Difficulty predicting which crops will be in demand.
6.Limited digital access to agricultural markets for small-scale farmers.

## Proposed Solution
Our solution is an AI-powered agricultural marketplace that connects farmers directly with households, retailers, restaurants, supermarkets, and wholesalers.

Unique Features:

1.Smart Price Predictor: Uses market trends and historical prices to suggest competitive selling prices for farmers.
2.Crop-to-Customer Matching: Recommends suitable buyers to farmers based on crop type, quantity, location, and demand.
3.Pre-Order Farming: Allows buyers to order crops before harvest, helping farmers plan production based on expected demand.
4.Food Rescue Alerts: Notifies nearby buyers when farmers have surplus produce that needs to be sold quickly.
5.AI Demand Forecasting: Predicts future demand for different agricultural products to reduce overproduction and shortages.
6.Shared Transport System: Groups orders from nearby farms to reduce delivery costs and improve vehicle utilization.
7.Freshness Tracking: Displays harvest dates and estimated freshness information to help buyers make informed purchasing decisions.
8.Multilingual Voice Assistant: Allows farmers to search for buyers, check prices, and manage orders using their preferred language.

## Technical Approach
The platform combines web and mobile technologies with AI-based prediction and logistics management.

Proposed Technologies:

1.Frontend: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and React.
2.Backend: Python with Django or Flask.
3.Database: MySQL or PostgreSQL.
4.Artificial Intelligence: Python, Pandas, and Scikit-learn.
5.Maps and Routing: Mapping APIs for distance calculation and route planning.
6.Communication: SMS or messaging notifications for order updates.

Implementation Process:

1.Farmers register and upload details of their available produce.
2.Buyers search for products by location, price, quantity, and quality.
3.The system matches farmers with suitable buyers.
4.AI estimates demand using historical sales and seasonal patterns.
5.The logistics module groups orders and suggests efficient delivery routes.
6.Buyers track orders and provide feedback after delivery.

Prototype Workflow:

Farmer Registration → Product Listing → Buyer Matching → AI Demand Prediction → Order Confirmation → Route Optimization → Delivery → Feedback.
## Feasibility and Viability
The project can initially be developed as a small web application and expanded into a complete marketplace.

Feasibility:

1.Uses widely available web development tools and open-source libraries.
2.Can begin with a limited group of farmers and buyers in one region.
3.Requires no specialized hardware for the initial prototype.
4.AI features can be introduced gradually as transaction data becomes available.

Potential Challenges and Solutions:
1.Low digital literacy: Provide a simple interface with local-language support and voice input.
2.Unreliable demand predictions: Start with basic historical data and improve the model as more data becomes available.
3.Transportation costs: Combine orders from nearby buyers and coordinate shared deliveries.
4.Product quality disputes: Introduce product descriptions, harvest dates, photographs, and buyer feedback.
5.Farmer participation: Work with FPOs and local agricultural groups to onboard sellers.
6.Limited initial funding: Launch a minimum viable product with essential features before adding advanced AI.

Business Model:

1.Small commission on successfully completed transactions.
2.Optional subscription plans for bulk buyers.
3.Logistics coordination fees.
4.Premium market insights for commercial buyers.
5.Basic marketplace access could remain free for farmers, helping encourage participation.
